## The rules

A **lesson** needs an argument and a program that demonstrates it, with no invented data or with data that says it is invented and has only the right shape. An **evidence-graded page** is for a claim no program can check; it needs a grade and a source for every claim, and it gives no doses, no supplement or drug advice and no medical advice. A topic that fits neither stays here.

## Lessons with an obvious program, not yet written

- **Procrastination as hyperbolic discounting.** A reward's value falls with delay, and falls faster near the present than far from it, so tonight's essay loses to tonight's film while next week's essay beats next week's film. A program can compute the crossing and show why a commitment made in advance (a scheduled block, the buffer hour) works when a resolution made at the time does not. Zakrajsek's chapter 2 names avoiding procrastination as a part of self-regulation, and this is the arithmetic under it.
- **Elaboration.** The fourth of the big four has no lesson because it has no obvious program. A candidate: the number of retrieval routes to a fact as a graph, and how many cues survive when each edge fails with some probability. It would need honest modelling to avoid inventing the result.
- **Pretesting.** A learner who guesses first against one who reads first, in the same counting style as [learned helplessness](03_Believing_You_Can/learned_helplessness/README.md). The mechanism is under-specified, so this needs care.
- **Feedback timing and the hypercorrection effect.** A program can at least show what a proper scoring rule says about high-confidence errors: they cost most, so correcting them gains most. That would join [metacognition](01_Knowing_What_You_Know/metacognition/README.md) to the finding.
- **Note-taking**: Mueller and Oppenheimer's typists versus writers, and the replication that shrank it. A program could count how much of a lecture transcript fits at typing speed against handwriting speed, which is the mechanism they proposed.

## Not planned as lessons

- **Group study, office hours, test-taking tactics** (McGuire's strategies 13, 27–32, 34, 35): advice with no program to show it. The [strategies table](01_Knowing_What_You_Know/README.md#mcguires-strategies-and-where-they-are-explained) lists them.
- **Sleep stages, exercise dose, diet and supplements as protocols.** The evidence for anything finer than the graded pages already say is weak, and a page that told a reader when to exercise or what to take would be pretending otherwise.
- **Gestalt laws of perception** (Zakrajsek's chapter 6): real, but about perception rather than study, and hard to make a claim about that a program tests.
