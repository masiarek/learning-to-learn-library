# Brain chemistry: what dopamine, acetylcholine and norepinephrine do, and what follows for a learner

**Level:** 101 · for anyone who has read that curiosity "releases dopamine" and wondered what to do with that

**One line:** The synapse strengthens when it is used (LTP), attention is sharpened by acetylcholine, novelty and reward by dopamine, arousal by norepinephrine: good evidence, most of it in animals. Almost nothing a learner can do follows from it that the study techniques did not already say, and every product sold on this chemistry is standing on the weak end of the evidence.

## The cellular story

**Long-term potentiation (LTP)** is the lasting strengthening of a synapse after it fires repeatedly, first recorded in the rabbit hippocampus by Bliss and Lømo in 1973: "neurons that fire together wire together" is Hebb's 1949 guess, and LTP is the mechanism that fits it. Blocking LTP blocks learning in animals; single-cell studies now trace the genes involved (Sardoo and colleagues, 2022, [doi:10.1093/bib/bbac412 ↗](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbac412)). *Grade: well established as a mechanism in animals; good evidence that it underlies human memory.* What follows for a learner: repetition that makes the synapse fire, which means retrieval, not rereading. That is [spaced retrieval](../../02_Making_It_Stay/spaced_retrieval/README.md), and the chemistry adds nothing to the advice.

## Three neuromodulators

| Modulator | What it does, in the evidence | Grade | What the evidence is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **acetylcholine** | raises the signal-to-noise ratio in cortex during attention and marks synapses for change; its loss is an early sign of Alzheimer's | good evidence | Decker and Duncan's review (2020, [doi:10.1016/j.cobeha.2020.01.013 ↗](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cobeha.2020.01.013)); Slater and colleagues (2022, [doi:10.3390/brainsci12070890 ↗](https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci12070890)); much of it in rodents |
| **dopamine** | tracks reward, novelty and prediction error; makes hippocampal plasticity easier, so what is rewarding or surprising is remembered better | good evidence | Duncan and Shohamy's handbook chapter (2024, [doi:10.1093/oxfordhb/9780190917982.013.24 ↗](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780190917982.013.24)); in people, Clos, Bunzeck and Sommer (2019, [doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0495-19.2019 ↗](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0495-19.2019)) and Gholston and Chiew (2023, [doi:10.1007/s00426-023-01829-5 ↗](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00426-023-01829-5)); curiosity's effect on memory, Gruber and Ranganath (2019, [doi:10.31219/osf.io/5v6nm ↗](https://doi.org/10.31219/osf.io/5v6nm)) |
| **norepinephrine** | sets arousal and alertness; moderate arousal at and just after learning helps consolidation, and chronic stress hurts it | good evidence | McGaugh's work on emotional memory; Tse and colleagues (2023, [doi:10.1073/pnas.2307275120 ↗](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2307275120)) in rats; Wolter and colleagues (2021, [doi:10.1007/s00213-021-05884-x ↗](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-021-05884-x)) in rats |

Several entries in the guide's reference list are animal or cell studies that support the table's mechanisms and nothing more: M4 receptor activation in rodents (Bubser and colleagues, 2014, [doi:10.1021/cn500128b ↗](https://doi.org/10.1021/cn500128b)); a dopamine agonist in rats (Proshin and colleagues, 2025, [doi:10.47056/0365-9615-2025-179-4-415-419 ↗](https://doi.org/10.47056/0365-9615-2025-179-4-415-419)); dopamine nanoparticles in mice (Liu and colleagues, 2026, [doi:10.7150/thno.121735 ↗](https://doi.org/10.7150/thno.121735)); alpha7 nicotinic agonists in rats (Bali and colleagues, 2019, [doi:10.3389/fphar.2019.00073 ↗](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2019.00073); Nagy and colleagues, 2024, [doi:10.1080/17590914.2024.2371160 ↗](https://doi.org/10.1080/17590914.2024.2371160)); flexibility training raising cortical dopamine in rats (Chaby and colleagues, 2019, [doi:10.3389/fnbeh.2019.00024 ↗](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnbeh.2019.00024)); a putative enhancer in rat dopamine neurons (Sagheddu and colleagues, 2020, [doi:10.3390/biom10050779 ↗](https://doi.org/10.3390/biom10050779)); deep-brain stimulation of the cholinergic system in monkeys (Bava and colleagues, 2023, [doi:10.3390/brainsci13060917 ↗](https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci13060917)). They are good science and they are not advice.

## What follows for a learner

The honest list is short, and every item on it was already in chapters 01 to 04 for other reasons:

1. **Attend.** Acetylcholine is the mechanism; "put the phone in another room" is the advice, and [cognitive load](../../02_Making_It_Stay/cognitive_load/README.md) has the arithmetic.
2. **Be curious, and make it matter.** What is surprising or wanted is remembered better. Asking the question before reading the answer, the pretesting effect on [how memory works](../how_memory_works/README.md), is the cheapest way to make an answer surprising.
3. **Moderate arousal, not none and not panic.** Norepinephrine's curve is an inverted U. [Mindset and motivation](../mindset_and_motivation/README.md) has what to do about the panic end.
4. **Retrieve, don't reread.** LTP needs the synapse to fire.

## What does not follow

- **"Dopamine detox", "dopamine fasting", "hack your dopamine".** Dopamine is not a substance you can run down and refill by abstaining from your phone; the phrase is a metaphor that has escaped. *Grade: speculative.*
- **Any supplement "for acetylcholine" or "for dopamine".** See [food and supplements](../food_and_supplements/README.md). *Grade: weak to none.*
- **90-minute ultradian focus blocks.** The 90-minute cycle is Kleitman's old hypothesis about sleep extended to waking by analogy; the evidence that attention runs on it is weak. Blocks of an hour or so with a real break are sensible for other reasons. *Grade: weak.*
- **Prescription stimulants for healthy learners.** Small, inconsistent gains and real harms; see [food and supplements](../food_and_supplements/README.md#prescription-stimulants-and-smart-drugs). *Grade: good evidence that it is a poor trade.*

## Po polsku, w skrócie

Synapsa wzmacnia się, gdy jest używana (długotrwałe wzmocnienie synaptyczne, LTP); acetylocholina wyostrza uwagę, dopamina znakuje nowość i nagrodę, noradrenalina ustawia pobudzenie. To dobre dowody, w większości ze zwierząt. Dla ucznia wynika z nich mało, czego nie mówiły już techniki z rozdziałów 1–4: uważaj, bądź ciekaw, nie panikuj, przypominaj sobie zamiast czytać ponownie. Nie wynika z nich „detoks dopaminowy”, suplementy „na acetylocholinę” ani 90-minutowe cykle uwagi; to metafory i słabe hipotezy. Każdy produkt sprzedawany na tej chemii stoi na słabym końcu dowodów.
