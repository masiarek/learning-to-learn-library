# 01_Knowing_What_You_Know — how do you know that you know?

**Level:** 101 · for anyone who has walked out of an exam sure it went well

Every other chapter here tells you what to do. This one comes first because it tells you how to check whether it worked. A feeling of knowing is not knowing, and the three lessons measure the gap between the two, show that what you remember depends on what you did with the material rather than how long you looked at it, and name the levels of doing. It follows Saundra McGuire's *Teach Yourself How to Learn*, chapters 3 and 4, and like every chapter it backs each claim with a program that runs.

| # | Lesson | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Metacognition: judging what you know](metacognition/README.md) | Of Flavell's four abilities, which can be measured, and why does rereading leave you sure and wrong? |
| 2 | [Count the vowels](count_the_vowels/README.md) | Why do people remember 3 of 15 phrases after one task and 12 of 15 after another, with nothing else changed? |
| 3 | [Studying vs learning](studying_vs_learning/README.md) | What are Bloom's six levels, and what does climbing them on one theorem buy you? |

## The through-line

Lesson 1 asks you to write a confidence before every answer and compare it with the result. The score it uses, the Brier score, is smallest when you report exactly what you believe, so it rewards nothing but honesty, and it shows the reader who reread their notes as sure and wrong. Lesson 2 is McGuire's exercise: fifteen phrases, and recall goes from 3 to 12 with nothing changed but the goal and an organising principle. Lesson 3 climbs Bloom's levels on the Pythagorean theorem, from stating it to building Euclid's formula, which regenerates every fact a student would otherwise memorise. The habit all three teach is checking, and the rest of the library assumes it: [02_Making_It_Stay](../02_Making_It_Stay/README.md) says how to keep what the check says you have, and [03_Believing_You_Can](../03_Believing_You_Can/README.md) turns the same check on beliefs about yourself.

## Po polsku, w skrócie

Ten rozdział jest pierwszy, bo uczy sprawdzać, czy nauka zadziałała. Uczucie, że się coś wie, nie jest wiedzą. Lekcja 1 mierzy różnicę: zapisz pewność przed odpowiedzią i porównaj z wynikiem; kto tylko czyta notatki drugi raz, jest pewny i często się myli. Lekcja 2 to ćwiczenie McGuire z liczeniem samogłosek: pamiętamy to, co robiliśmy z materiałem, a nie to, na co patrzyliśmy. Lekcja 3 opisuje poziomy Blooma na twierdzeniu Pitagorasa, od podania wzoru po zbudowanie wzoru Euklidesa, który odtwarza wszystkie fakty. Nawyk, którego uczą wszystkie trzy, to sprawdzanie.

## A note on the code

The learner in lesson 1 is invented, and says so; the Brier score and why honesty minimises it are exact. Lesson 2's vowel count and 15! are exact, and lesson 3 checks Euclid's formula against brute force. The books behind the chapter are on the [resources](../RESOURCES.md) page.
