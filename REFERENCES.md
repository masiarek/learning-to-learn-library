# References

**Level:** reference · for checking what a claim on the graded pages rests on

**One line:** The seventy-one papers cited by the guide this library was asked to absorb, each with a link and a note on what kind of study it is, because a meta-analysis of classroom experiments and a study of mice given an amnesia drug do not weigh the same.

The list arrived as a numbered bibliography attached to a guide on learning. The numbers are kept so the guide can be checked against it. The pages in [05_Body_and_Brain](05_Body_and_Brain/README.md) cite the ones that bear on a claim; the rest are here so that nothing is silently dropped. The tag after each entry is this library's reading of it: **review** (systematic review, meta-analysis or handbook chapter), **classroom** (an experiment or study on students), **lab** (an experiment on people in a laboratory), **animal** (rodents or other animals), **theory**, **preprint** (not yet peer reviewed), **correction** (an erratum, not a finding), **off-topic** (not about learning, or not recoverable from the citation).

## What the sorting shows

Of seventy-one entries, about twenty-five are reviews or classroom studies on the techniques in chapters 01 to 04, and they agree with those chapters. Thirteen are animal or cell studies, which is where nearly every sentence in the guide about neurotransmitters, supplements and "priming" comes from. Six are preprints or corrections, and eight are not about the claim they were attached to. That distribution is the reason [05_Body_and_Brain](05_Body_and_Brain/README.md) grades every claim: the guide's confident tone was the same in every section, and its evidence was not.
